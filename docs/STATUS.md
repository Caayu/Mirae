## Current limitations

See [TECHNICAL_DEBT.md](TECHNICAL_DEBT.md) for the full list with
priorities. Most relevant: no physical tablet validation; 8-bit default
storage and 8-bit masks (16-bit documents optional), 8-bit display;
document size bounded by GPU texture size; undo history uncompressed in memory;
saving and loading synchronous (no autosave, recent files or background
save); no layer thumbnails or folders (debt #17);
CPU composition single-threaded and linear in layers (TECHNICAL_DEBT #33);
no transparent colour, palette not persisted (debt #35, #36); transforms of
whole large layers preview slowly on one core (debt #40); no gap closing
or area scaling in fills (debt #42); the reference's default gradient
mixing not reproduced and large gradient previews spread over frames
(debt #43); preset edits not persisted; UI unpolished.

## Phase 5F outcome

Gradients, with the reference observed first
([parity/5F-gradients.md](parity/5F-gradients.md), GD1–GD38; 40 decoded
captures kept as test data). Probes of our own, reduced to column, ring
and angle means, showed: every segment between stops eased by
`E(u) = 0.38u + 0.62q(u)` (193 of 211 undithered integers exact); its
Standard mixing on sRGB-encoded values, Linear in linear light, Perceptual
(Photoshop-compatible) in OKLab; its default "Perceptiva" with a
brightness correction matching no public space; straight colour and
alpha, transparent stops carrying no colour; four end rules with
anti-aliased wraps and edges; a deterministic ordered one-level dither
biased half a level; source-over, source-atop with alpha lock, the
selection limiting and scaling, masks by alpha, layer properties not
baked, one step, a guide line only during the drag. Mirae (ADR 0027):
`tela_color::gradient` (stops, the easing, three mixes, a per-segment
`RampTable` within half a 16-bit level), `tela_document::gradient`
(linear, radial, elliptical, angular, reflected; clamp, repeat, reflect,
empty), its own mean-preserving 8 × 8 dither at the storage's precision,
one per-pixel function for a live preview (bit-identical to the commit)
and `Document::apply_gradient` (one step); the app's gradient tool (G
cycles fill and gradient, Shift, Escape cancels, the eraser end), a
panel with a stop editor and a drag guide. The reference's three public
mixes are reproduced within 1.5 levels at both depths, circles, the
ellipse, repeat and reverse within ~1 px of position, alpha, transparent
stops, alpha lock, selections and masks within 1.5–2 levels; property
tests compare with an independent `f64` model; gradients joined the
randomized sessions. Verified in the running app (mouse and synthetic
pen): all 20 session checks, including cancel, undo/redo, a rotated
view, 16-bit, save/reopen and export. Costs: a 2048² preview frame ~100
ms and commit ~80 ms; 4096² ~0.4 s and ~0.3 s (angular twice that); the
app spreads large previews over frames (on-screen tiles first, ≤ 12 ms
per frame). Remaining: D-5F-1…10 (the reference's default mixing, the
colour phase, circle/ellipse drag mapping, dither pattern, options,
large previews).

## Phase 5E outcome

Fill, with the reference observed first ([parity/5E-fill.md](parity/5E-fill.md),
BK1–BK35; its measured classifications and decoded captures kept as test
data). Probes of our own (1 568 tolerance islands, a one-level chain,
three transparency zones with hidden colours, a multi-layer stack with a
paper layer) showed one rule with 0 misfits on 31 captures: a 4-connected
flood compared with the seed's colour by the largest straight channel
difference, the alpha difference alone where either pixel is transparent,
included up to `min(round(2.55 t), 254)` levels; anti-aliasing covers the
4-adjacent excluded ring by `(1 − d)/(1 − T)`; "refer to other layers" is
the visible composite without the paper; the selection limits
connectivity and its coverage scales the fill. Mirae reproduces it
(ADR 0026): `Document::fill_region`, `fill_connected`, `fill_selection`,
the reference separate from the target, the brush commit's paint rules,
one history step (none for a no-op), a sparse span flood over per-tile
`u16` distance maps (nothing materialized; only changed tiles written).
Every island and edge matches at 16 bits; at 8 bits two translucent
islands on the 30 % threshold differ (premultiplied storage, D-5E-1).
Property tests compare the flood with an independent breadth-first
search; fills joined the history model and the randomized sessions,
whose stroke oracle now compares up to the stroked layer under blend
layers (an 8-bit amplification the new sequences exposed, not a defect).
Verified in the running app with the mouse and the synthetic pen at 8
and 16 bits, zoomed and rotated, with masks, clipping, selections and
alpha lock, through save/reopen and export. Costs: small regions 2–5 ms,
a whole empty 4096² layer ~32 ms, a 4096² background through the
composite ~0.27 s. Remaining: D-5E-1…9 (no gap closing or area scaling,
other fill options, no-op steps, the transparent colour, speed not
compared).

## Phase 5D outcome

Transform, with the reference observed first ([parity/5D-transform.md](parity/5D-transform.md),
T1–T45; its measured layers kept as test data). A probe of our own read
over white and over black gave the transformed layer's premultiplied
pixels exactly, and the reference's default resampler was fitted on
every pixel: Keys' cubic with `a = −1` on premultiplied colour, a 2 × 2
pyramid while both scales are ≤ ½, the sample clamped to the edge pixels,
un-premultiplied by the raw alpha, whole-pixel edges when axis-aligned
and distance-anti-aliased sides when rotated; nearest neighbour ties to
the lower pixel. Mirae reproduces it (ADR 0025): scales within 3/255
(Mirae stores premultiplied, the reference straight 8-bit), nearest
neighbour, flips and moves exactly, rotations inside a one-pixel edge
ring. The transform is an explicit session in the app (the document does
not change until the commit, which is one history step; a cancel changes
nothing); with a selection `p·s` moves over `p·(1 − s)` and the selection
follows; the mask moves with its layer; hidden, locked and empty layers
are refused as in the reference; off-canvas pixels are kept. Preview and
commit call one per-tile function (bit-exact after the single
quantization, both depths). Verified in the running app with the mouse
and the synthetic pen at 8 and 16 bits, zoomed and rotated, through
save/reopen and export. Costs: a few ms for common selections, 70–285 ms
per change for a whole 2048² layer, ~1 s for 4096² (debt #40).
Remaining: D-5D-1…11 (rotated edge ring and selection corners, bilinear
and "average colours" modes, distortions out of scope, large previews).

## Phase 5C outcome

Selections, with the reference observed first ([parity/5C-selection.md](parity/5C-selection.md),
S1–S32; its measured canvas images kept as test data). The selection is
document state (ADR 0024): an 8-bit coverage stored sparse as
`coverage XOR fill`, so Select All and Invert cost nothing on any
canvas; clipped to the canvas; an empty result is no selection, as in the
reference. Rectangle, ellipse and lasso tools with the reference's
modifiers and anti-aliasing defaults; the reference's operators (exact on
its own inputs) and feather (within 2/255 from 1 to 50 px); strokes,
erasing and mask strokes multiplied by the coverage in the one rule
preview and commit share (exact against the reference's strokes; 0
allocations per dab, preview cost unchanged); every change a swap-based
history step; not saved (the reference does not save it either; `.mirae`
v1 unchanged). Verified in the running app with the mouse and the
synthetic pen, at 8 and 16 bits, zoomed and rotated. The baseline run
found two pre-existing defects from 5B in random property tests (a
history reservation for merged renames; a test model), fixed before 5C
started. Remaining: D-5C-1…8 (ellipse edge pixels, lasso smoothing, four
AA levels, unimplemented sub-tools, Ctrl+Shift+I held by another
program on this machine).

## Phase 5B outcome

Layer workflow with the reference observed first ([parity/5B-transparent-compositing.md](parity/5B-transparent-compositing.md)).
D-4C-1 resolved: a probe of 45 900 translucent-backdrop combinations
without paper showed one rule (Multiply W3C; the other modes compose the
blended colour over the backdrop first; additive alpha for Screen, Color
Dodge, Hard Light, Soft Light), now Mirae's (ADR 0022) within 2/255;
composition over the paper is bit-identical to before and as fast
(A/B). Merge down (ADR 0023) as the reference does it: the pair composed
alone, the lower layer keeps id, name, mode, clipping and alpha lock,
opacity baked, the lower-mask question (Apply / Keep / Cancel), refused
on hidden or locked layers or without a layer below, one undo step
holding only the changed tiles; 22 measured scenarios within 2/255 at
both depths. Rename by double-click (consecutive renames are one step,
as in the reference), drag-and-drop reorder with an insertion line,
Ctrl+E, a fixed-width panel. Transparent export of blend layers is no
longer refused. Merge 4096² takes 111–247 ms; undo/redo < 0.1 ms.
Remaining: D-5B-1…9 (merging onto the paper, Normal over 1/255, 8-bit
dodge/burn precision, no thumbnails, other merge commands).
